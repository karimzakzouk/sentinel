# Decision log

Each decision lists what was chosen, why, and what evidence would change it.
The design canvases in `docs/plan/` hold the decisions made before code; this
file records the ones made while building, including where building proved the
plan wrong.

| 17 | The count detector's significance level comes straight from the false-alarm budget: alpha = budget ÷ (ticks per week × fact kinds). | 18 | Events that happened before the controller started are not facts. The informer's initial list, and any event last seen before start, only set the baseline. | After a restart, an hour of old readiness-probe failures counted as new and opened false incidents. | Sentinel must explain incidents that began before it started. |
| 19 | Incidents are keyed by workload and resolve after a quiet gap (`detection.incidentGap`, default 5 minutes) with no open episode. | 20 | Only a workload's latest template change is credited with failing pods on the newest revision; its older template changes are credited with failures on older revisions. | A revert is itself the latest change. Crediting every template change with the newest revision's failures let the revert take the blame for the incident it was fixing. | — |
| 21 | While any series of a workload is in an episode, none of that workload's series learns. | During an OOM incident, the crash-loop facts were too sparse to be significant on their own. Freezing only the alerting series let them leak into the crash-loop baseline, so a later crash loop looked normal. | Long incidents starve unrelated series of learning; then freeze only the series that move with the alerting one. |
| 22 | A container that exited non-zero, in a pod that will restart it, counts as crash-looping whatever the back-off status says. | On Kubernetes 1.37 (kind v0.33), a crashing container spends most of its back-off as terminated with reason Error, and shows CrashLoopBackOff only briefly. Counting only CrashLoopBackOff missed the crash-on-start scenario. | — |
| 23 | The classifier sees every fact kind in the workload's window, not only the significant ones, and only changes made since the workload's last incident resolved. | An OOM incident opened as crash-on-start: the single OOM kill was not significant on its own, and the previous scenario's image change and revert still counted as recent changes. | Calibration (slice 6) shows sub-threshold facts mislead more than they help. |
| 24 | Log access is its own capability (`detect.logs`); without it Sentinel watches from Kubernetes facts alone. | Reading logs was part of the watcher's required permissions, so a cluster denying `pods/log` lost all detection. | — |
| 25 | Logs are fetched on triggers within a budget (30 a minute, two at a time, 300 lines and 64 KiB each), summarized and redacted where they land, and recorded only when they join an incident. | Raw lines must never reach the permanent Ledger, and logs of workloads that never have an incident need not be kept at all. | 26 | "New error after deploy" compares the new revision's first minutes with the old revision's last five, sampled before the rollout replaces it. It tests a rate ratio above 2, not above 1, at 10⁻³ per rollout with Bonferroni across the errors tested. | Faults that leave pods ready (a misspelled dependency) are invisible to Kubernetes facts. | 27 | Scenario runs on the development cluster use a one-minute detection window. | Short windows make incidents resolve soon after a revert, so scenarios run back to back. The cost: a fault that recurs slower than the window, like an OOM kill every two minutes, never puts two facts in one window and is missed. The default five-minute window catches it; slice 4's persistence detectors are the real answer. | Slice 4 lands, or the scoring slice needs the production window. |
| 28 | A Service losing every ready endpoint is a fact on the workloads behind it, but only after Sentinel has seen it with endpoints. | Scaling Redis to zero fails no pod; the empty Service is the only Kubernetes-level trace, and it names the cause directly. Requiring prior endpoints keeps Services that are empty by design (scaled-to-zero environments) from alarming at startup. | Intentional scale-downs alarm too often; then weigh who made the change. |
| 29 | The dependency map starts from configuration: literal environment values and arguments naming a Service. | 30 | Undoing a culprit depends on its kind: a rollback for a template change, `scale.restore` for a scale change. | Rolling back a Deployment's template does not bring back replicas a scale change removed. | — |
| 31 | `brain.localize` ranks root causes from evidence ratios (own anomaly, upstream position, recent change, a healthy dependency's unlikelihood); grouping a downstream symptom into its upstream incident waits for slice 4. | Every current scenario raises its incident on the root cause, so grouping could not yet be tested live; slice 4's log-rate detectors produce the downstream symptoms it needs. | — |
| 32 | The scorer (slice 6) and the read-only UI (slice 5) came before the rate detectors (slice 4). | The gate is a measurement: without `harness score`, no change to the detectors could be shown to help or hurt, and every later slice is judged by it. | 33 | A detection on a workload joins the open incident of a workload it depends on when localization ranks that workload first; an incident whose cause turns out to be in another incident merges into it. | 34 | The UI and its API are read-only and listen on loopback inside the pod; `kubectl port-forward` is the way in. | 35 | Incidents left open when the controller stops are taken back when it starts, not closed: each starts quiet and resolves after the incident gap unless its workloads' trouble returns. | 36 | Every minute, each workload's errors are sampled from one ready pod's last minute of logs, rotating through its pods, as low-priority fetches within the log budget; each error record is a `log.errors` fact. Error series need at least twice their expected count as well as significance to alarm, end when back under 1.5 times it, take a weak prior instead of the pooled one, learn for 30 minutes before they may alarm, and are learned anew after an hour at one level. | Faults that leave pods up (a dependency gone, traffic finding a bug) were invisible outside rollouts. One pod at a time keeps the count a per-pod rate that scaling does not change, and costs one fetch per workload a minute. Error volumes are large and bursty: with much history the Gamma–Poisson predictive is near Poisson, so a fifth more errors would be significant; a workload's usual level is its own, not the near-zero the pooled prior expects; and an episode on a level that holds would never end, freezing the workload's baselines. | The soak's false alarms come from error series; then model overdispersion and seasonality (SPOT, as slice 4 plans) instead of the ratio floor. |
| 37 | A new class, `runtime.errors`: errors at an unusual rate with pods up and no change to blame. Sentinel escalates it. | Without it, an error-only incident would be forced into a crash or deploy class and could be rolled back for nothing. | — |

| 48 | The Lab runs as its own identity, separate from the controller and the actor. | 51 | Each world gets a Lab DNS: CoreDNS serving one authoritative root zone with each copied Service's short and production names at its Lab address, so copies' configuration resolves to the Lab unchanged; every other name gets NXDOMAIN, and each query is logged. | 52 | A Lab verdict rests on three e-processes per candidate, each a product of bets valid however long the Lab runs and whenever it stops: *better*, over blocks where the baseline and the candidate disagree, against "no better than the failing revision"; *works* and *fails*, over the candidate's own runs, against "works at most" and "at least three times in four". PASS needs *better* and *works* at α over the K candidates, once the failure has reproduced and every control has failed; FAIL needs *fails*; anything else at the budget is NOT SURE. | The Lab decides when to stop as evidence arrives, which breaks fixed-sample tests; Ville's inequality bounds an e-process at every stopping time. A test of disagreeing blocks alone never moves when the candidate fails exactly like an always-failing baseline, so it could never FAIL one; the candidate's own record can. Requiring both tests to reject keeps PASS within its share of α without another split. Simulated: a clear fix passes in 13 to 15 blocks, a non-fix fails in 3, and at the edge of the null 3.4% of candidates pass at α = 5%. | The self-hosted runner's repetitions show the false-PASS rate above α on real workloads, or flaky baselines need a bound other than three in four. |
| 53 | A run's oracle: the copy must become ready within a minute and then stay ready for twenty seconds without a restart.
